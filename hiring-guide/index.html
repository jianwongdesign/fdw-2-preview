Hiring questions

Think through a busy evening at home. Who plans dinner, what time is everyone back, and where would an extra pair of hands help most? Bring that example to your first conversation.

Getting started

Is 1.Alpha licensed, and which countries do you work with?

Our employment agency licence number is 21C0788. We recruit in Indonesia, Myanmar and the Philippines. Check 1.Alpha in the EA Directory · See all MOM-approved source countries and regions

What should I prepare as a first-time employer?

For our first chat, bring your questions and a rough outline of the help you need. Before applying, first-time employers generally attend the Employers’ Orientation Programme. The supporting documents depend on your circumstances and application route. Check MOM’s application steps and documents

How long will it take for a helper to arrive?

Share your preferred start date when we speak. An overseas hire needs time for matching, the Work Permit application, approval and travel. We can give you a placement estimate once we know whom you plan to hire. See MOM’s Work Permit timeline

Fees, salary and levy

What will I pay before and after placement?

We explain the agency fees and any placement loan before you agree to hire. For your ongoing budget, allow for your helper’s salary, levy, food, insurance and medical care. Ask us for an itemised estimate for your household.

What salary should I expect for a helper from Indonesia, Myanmar or the Philippines?

Ask us for a current figure for the helper you are considering. Experience, agreed duties and any requirements from her home country can affect the offer. Agree the salary together and record it in the employment contract. See MOM’s contract guidance

How much is the monthly levy?

The standard rate is S$300 for the first helper and S$450 for each subsequent helper. Eligible households can pay S$60 a month under the concession. Check the current levy rates · Check concession eligibility

Insurance and medical care

What is the S$5,000 bond? Do I have to lodge cash?

For a non-Malaysian helper, the employer arranges a S$5,000 security bond before arrival. A bank or insurer can issue the guarantee. The bond is separate from your agency fees and the employer pays for it. Read MOM’s security bond conditions

Which insurance policies do I need?

Arrange medical insurance and personal accident insurance before arrival. Medical cover must provide at least S$60,000 a year for inpatient care and day surgery. The accident policy must insure at least S$60,000 a year for accidental death or permanent disability. Check MOM’s insurance requirements

When are medical checks due, and who pays?

Arrange the pre-employment medical examination within two weeks of an overseas helper’s arrival. During employment, follow MOM’s six-monthly screening notices. The employer covers required examinations and medical treatment. Read about the arrival medical check · Read about six-monthly screening

Once your helper is home

How can my helper send money home?

She can choose a bank or licensed cross-border money transfer provider. Compare the fee, exchange rate and amount her family will receive before sending. Find providers in the MAS directory

Who can we speak to if we need help settling in?

Contact our Bukit Timah team. Tell us what’s going well and what has been difficult, whether that’s understanding instructions, a change in routine or agreeing priorities. Contact 1.Alpha
